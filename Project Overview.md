# Quick Commerce Control Room

### Profitability × Fulfillment × Customer × Inventory Intelligence

## What is this project?

The **Quick Commerce Control Room** is an end-to-end business analytics case study designed to simulate how a quick-commerce company can use data to monitor, analyze, and manage its core business operations from a centralized control-room framework.

The project brings together four critical business dimensions — **Profitability, Fulfillment, Customer, and Inventory Intelligence** — and connects them with an additional **Audit & Management Decision layer**.

Instead of looking at individual reports or KPIs separately, the project creates a unified framework that moves from:

**Data → Analysis → Intelligence → Alerts → Management Decisions**

The purpose is to demonstrate how operational data can be transformed into actionable business insights and decision-support mechanisms.

---

## Why does it matter?

Quick Commerce operates in a highly time-sensitive and cost-sensitive environment where **speed, product availability, customer experience, and profitability must be balanced simultaneously**.

A business may achieve high order volumes and revenue while still facing:

* High fulfillment costs
* Low contribution margins
* Inventory stock-outs
* Overstocking
* Delivery delays
* Order cancellations
* Inefficient operations
* Customer dissatisfaction

Therefore, monitoring a single metric such as revenue or order volume is not sufficient.

The Control Room approach provides management with a consolidated view of **what is happening, where the problem is occurring, how serious it is, and what action should be considered.**

This makes the project relevant to **Business Analytics, Operations Management, Product Operations, Business Intelligence, and Data-Driven Decision Making.**

---

## What does it cover?

The project covers five interconnected intelligence areas:

### 💰 1. Profitability Intelligence

Evaluates whether business growth is translating into sustainable financial performance.

Key areas include:

* Revenue
* Order economics
* Discounts
* Product costs
* Fulfillment costs
* Delivery costs
* Contribution margin
* Cost per order
* Product/category profitability

---

### 🚚 2. Fulfillment Intelligence

Evaluates how efficiently customer orders move through the fulfillment process.

Key areas include:

* Order processing
* Picking and packing
* Dispatch
* Delivery time
* SLA performance
* Delayed orders
* Cancellation
* Peak-hour operational pressure

---

### 📦 3. Inventory Intelligence

Evaluates whether the business has the right products available at the right time.

Key areas include:

* Stock availability
* Demand
* Stock-outs
* Overstock
* Fast-moving SKUs
* Slow-moving SKUs
* Inventory risk
* Replenishment requirements

---

### 👥 4. Customer Intelligence

Analyzes customer behavior and its relationship with business performance.

Key areas include:

* New vs. returning customers
* Order frequency
* Average Order Value
* Customer segments
* Purchase patterns
* Cancellation behavior
* Peak ordering periods

---

### 🛡️ 5. Audit & Management Decision Intelligence

Adds a decision-oriented layer to the analysis by identifying conditions that require management attention.

The framework considers:

* Critical inventory conditions
* SLA violations
* High cancellation rates
* Low-margin orders
* Operational exceptions
* Abnormal conditions
* Business risks

These conditions can then be classified into **Normal, Warning, and Critical** states to support prioritization and management action.
